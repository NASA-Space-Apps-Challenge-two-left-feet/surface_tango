## Problem Statement

The official challenge asks for an interactive application that turns NISAR radar observations into visible, actionable surface-change insight. BailaSAR builds that for three Colombian hazards, in the team's priority order:

1. **Landslides in the Aburrá Valley** (Medellín, Bello, Copacabana).
2. **Earthquake damage assessment and first-aid deployment** for the Aug 10 2026 M7.4 earthquake. The worst impacts were in the Eje Cafetero and Chocó.
3. **Flooding at La Mojana / Caregato.**

Optical sensors are blinded by cloud, and C-band decorrelates under canopy. NISAR L-band (λ = 24 cm) is the sensor that can see these processes.

The architecture is the one the team designed and code-generated in an earlier Gemini session:
- a LangGraph fan-out/fan-in over hazard agents;
- two FastMCP servers;
- a GEE fusion layer;
- a local Zarr v3 / COG cache behind a circuit breaker;
- a FastAPI SSE + MapLibre/Deck.gl mission control with a 3-panel optical / C-band / L-band curtain slider;
- a story-map tour and an interactive math-lab.

This doc keeps that architecture. It corrects every place where it rested on data or physics that this session showed to be false, and it moves anything outside the corrected scope to **TODO next tasks**.

## What Makes This Cool

- **The curtain slider is the "whoa."** On the same hillside or the same flooded forest, the optical panel shows cloud, the C-band panel shows decorrelated noise, and the L-band panel shows the signal. One drag of the slider makes the radar-physics argument with no words.
- **Each disaster is analyzed at home, by the people who live there.** The team presents in Medellín, about the Aburrá slopes, the coffee-region quake and La Mojana. That "hometown context" is what the challenge rewards.
- **First-aid ranking with a scorecard.** The quake chapter doesn't stop at a damage map. It ranks barrios for first-aid deployment, then scores the ranking against reported damage and against a population-only baseline. Few teams will show that a radar result beat a naive baseline, or honestly say that it didn't.
- **The agents explain; they never invent.** The LangGraph synthesis writes an ES/EN bulletin for UNGRD/DAGRD, and every number in it is rendered from a deterministic tool output or a sourced event constant.

## Premises (agreed this session)

1. **NISAR record window.** The public L-band archive covers acquisitions from **Jun 17 2026** onward. Validated L2 GCOV/GUNW were released Nov 6 2026, and earlier products may be *provisional*. Every granule records its version, and the UI labels provisional data.
2. **Build priority and headline.** The build priority is (1) Aburrá landslides, (2) the Aug 10 2026 quake, (3) La Mojana flood. **The 30-second video headlines the quake**, because it is the largest event inside the NISAR record and can be validated against published damage. Biomass is dropped from scope (TODO).
3. **Aburrá.** NISAR GUNW for Jun–Nov 2026 shows **currently** moving slopes. The Granizal (Bello) debris flow of **Jun 24 2025** predates NISAR and serves only as historical context and validation. If the velocity field doesn't beat the noise floor, the UI says so.
4. **Earthquake.** USGS gives M7.4 near San José del Palmar (Chocó) at a depth of **116 km** (USGS preferred-origin estimate 116.13 km, event us6000tjl2; displayed as `depth_km` 116 in `validation/event_facts.json`, labelled a USGS estimate). The SGC manual bulletin estimate is 105 km; an earlier 96 km figure had no source and was removed. A quake that deep has **no surface rupture**. The physics is a coherence-loss damage proxy map (pre-event pair vs co-event pair) over the affected cities (Pereira, Armenia, Manizales first). First-aid ranking: the frozen formula in Hazard 2 (built-up-pixel DPM × WorldPop × hospital distance; footprints only classify pixels). Murindó/Romeral and GOFF surface rupture move to TODO.
5. **Flood.** Over Colombia, L-band is **dual-pol HH/HV**, so the flood physics is HH change detection against a dry baseline plus HAND gating:
   - darker HH means open water;
   - brighter HH with a stable HV/HH ratio means flooded vegetation.

   The quad-pol thresholds (ᾱ > 52°, f_double-bounce > 0.55) are dropped unless a quad-pol granule actually covers the AOI. **S-band is out of the build:** it is distributed via ISRO Bhoonidhi, and no Colombia coverage was found.
6. **Agents route, tools compute.** LLM agents select tools and write bulletin prose with **placeholders**. They never produce a number. Numbers are rendered from tool-output keys (see Bulletin contract). The LLM response cache used by `make pitch` is keyed on a hash of the tool outputs. It is invalidated when they change and re-checked at serve time.
7. **No synthetic data, anywhere.** The radar synthesizer in the generated `prewarm_antioquia_cache.py` ("physically realistic radar synthesizer seeded by FABDEM + Sentinel-1") **is deleted**. The offline cache holds only real pre-staged tiles, each with its source granule ID. A missing pixel stays missing (NaN, rendered as "no data").

### Data layer

- **Hour-2 discovery gate:** `discover_nisar_colombia.py` runs for real over Jun 17 → Nov 14 2026. Its output, `nisar_colombia_granules_2026.json`, records per granule:
  - granule ID;
  - product (GCOV, GUNW);
  - acquisition dates (reference and secondary for GUNW);
  - track and frame;
  - polarizations;
  - version (provisional or validated);
  - whether the ionosphere phase-screen layer is present.

  The gate also records the **coherence layer's polarization and posting** for every GUNW.

  The quake needs three GUNW pairs over the Eje Cafetero, all on the same track:
  - **co-event (C):** dates span Aug 10;
  - **pre-event reference (R):** both dates before Aug 10, and the pair immediately precedes C;
  - **control (K):** both dates before R, with no date shared with R or C.

  All three pairs must have the same temporal baseline (12 days; if no exact match exists, the closest available, with the mismatch recorded). The **control pass** runs the identical DPM formula (Hazard 2) with K in the role of R and R in the role of C, i.e. on Δγ = γ_K − γ_R. Both of those pairs are pre-event, so no built-up excess is expected. A post-event control is used only if no pre-event K exists. It is then labelled post-event, and residual change from demolition, debris removal or aftershocks is expected and disclosed.

  The gate also records CHIRPS precipitation over the 3 days before each acquisition date. A wetness mismatch between R and C beyond 2× is disclosed next to the scorecard.

  **Gate decision table (hour 2), applied in order:**

Video wording drops "quake-specific". |

### Hazard 1: Aburrá landslides (`landslide_insar_math.py`, `landslide_agent`)

- **Phase pipeline:**
  - GUNW unwrapped phase.
  - Subtract the **provided** ionosphere phase screen.
  - Remove the stratified troposphere with a LOS-vs-elevation fit over stable valley-floor pixels (FABDEM).
  - Mask layover/shadow; keep the reference connected component.
  - d_LOS = −λΔφ/4π. Check the sign convention against the product spec.
  - Project to the downslope direction (FABDEM slope and aspect).
- **Honesty test:** keep a velocity only where |rate| > stable-pixel residual RMS **and** the sign agrees across two independent pairs. Otherwise publish "no motion detected above X mm noise floor (n pairs)."
- **HKLSI** (the team's index: kinematics + CHIRPS R15d + slope) is labelled **uncalibrated**, unless it is validated against an inventory (SGC SIMMA / DAGRD events) with hit rate and denominator.
- **Granizal 2025:** shown as a historical event marker. It is never presented as a NISAR detection.

### Hazard 2: Aug 10 2026 earthquake: damage and first aid (`earthquake_dpm_math.py`, `earthquake_agent`), the video headline

- **Damage proxy, per pixel, at the GUNW coherence posting (20 m expected):**
  - Δγ = γ_R − γ_C (unclamped).
  - μ and σ are the mean and SD of Δγ over all valid **non-built** pixels in the AOI on the same pair. Non-built pixels are the reference for change that isn't building damage.
  - Z = (Δγ − μ) / σ. The DPM is max(Z, 0), clamped after standardization.
  - The formula needs only R and C, so it is identical in every gate branch.
  - Mask vegetation (land cover), water and layover/shadow.
  - A pixel is **built-up** if footprint coverage (VIDA Combined or Google/Overture) is ≥ 50% of its area, and **non-built** if coverage is ≤ 10%.
  - Footprints only classify pixels. With ~10 m buildings and 20–80 m pixels, nothing is aggregated per building.
- **Unit of analysis: barrio** (official barrio/vereda polygons). A unit is evaluated only if it has ≥ 20 valid built-up pixels. Excluded units are counted and reported.
- **First-aid ranking (frozen formula):** P(x) is the percentile rank of x across evaluated units in the AOI, in [0, 1].
  - DPM_u = median Z of the built-up pixels in unit u.
  - pop_u = WorldPop sum in u.
  - dist_u = road distance from the unit centroid to the nearest hospital (OSM, extract date recorded). Farther from care means higher priority.
  - **Radar ranking:** score_u = P(DPM_u) × P(pop_u) × (1 + 0.25·P(dist_u)).
  - **Baseline ranking:** score_u = P(pop_u) × (1 + 0.25·P(dist_u)). This is the same formula with the radar term removed, so the comparison isolates radar.
  - Blocked roads are not in the scored ranking (TODO).
  - Output: ranked list plus GeoJSON.
- **Pre-registration (`validation/README.md`, committed before any C-pair coherence is viewed):**
  - the formula above and the evaluation unit;
  - the positive label: a barrio is "damaged" if the named source lists ≥ 1 damaged or collapsed building in it;
  - the ground-truth source, with name, date and access URL;
  - k = 10% of evaluated units (minimum 5);
  - the R / C / K pair IDs;
  - the comparison rule (below).
- **Scorecard:**
  - precision@k for radar vs baseline, each with a Wilson 95% CI;
  - the paired difference with a bootstrap 95% CI;
  - the control check: the same pipeline on K vs R, reporting precision@k and the median Z in built-up vs non-built pixels;
  - the built-up vs non-built median Z on C.

  **Built-up excess** on a pass is E = median Z(built-up pixels) − median Z(non-built pixels). All CIs use a **barrio-block bootstrap**: whole evaluated barrios are resampled with replacement, never individual pixels, because neighbouring coherence pixels are spatially autocorrelated. This runs in `validation/scoring.py`.

  **"Radar beats baseline"** only if all three hold:
  1. the paired precision@k difference CI excludes 0;
  2. E on C has a CI above 0;
  3. the control-adjusted excess (E_C − E_K, with K the pre-event control pass) has a CI above 0. This is a difference-in-differences test against the pre-event built-up vs non-built contrast, not a test that K shows no difference.

  K's precision@k is reported but does not enter the rule.
- **Informative scorecard:** the scorecard is computed only if all three hold:
  - ≥ 30 evaluated units;
  - ≥ 5 positives;
  - base-rate prevalence (positives ÷ evaluated units) between 10% and 70%.

  Prevalence is always shown next to precision@k. Otherwise the result is "uninformative (n units, n positives, prevalence p)", and the video shows the damage proxy map with that statement, not a precision number.
- **No barrio-level damage reports** means the scorecard is uninformative. There is no coarser-unit fallback.
- **If radar doesn't beat the baseline,** the headline becomes that measured comparison: "what radar added over knowing where people live." The claim "ready for UNGRD" is not made.

### Hazard 3: La Mojana / Caregato flood (`flood_polarimetric_math.py`, `flood_caregato_agent`)

- **Absolute open-water threshold:** HH γ0 < −18 dB. It is literature-typical for L-band and uncalibrated here.
- **Reference date:** the gate picks the **driest available NISAR GCOV date** in the window. That is the date with the lowest open-water fraction f (the share of valid HAND < 18 m pixels below the absolute threshold), with CHIRPS 15-day precipitation recorded.
  - If (f_wettest − f_driest) / f_wettest < 0.20, no acceptable dry baseline exists.
  - In that case, Class 2 ships as a documented null. Class 1 is reported as **absolute** low-backscatter water extent per date, worded "water extent on date X", with no change attribution.
  - No cross-sensor (Sentinel-1 or ALOS) baseline is used.
- **Classification:** GCOV HH and HV γ0 change against the reference date, gated by HAND < 18 m (MERIT Hydro).
  - **Class 1 (open water):** ΔHH_dB < −5.5 dB.
  - **Class 2 (flooded vegetation):** ΔHH_dB > +3 dB **and** |Δ(HV/HH)_dB| < 1.5 dB.
  - All five parameters (absolute −18 dB, −5.5 dB, +3 dB, 1.5 dB, HAND 18 m) are **literature-typical but uncalibrated here**, and the UI and bulletin label them that way.
  - The cache build also computes hectares at each dB threshold ±1 dB, and HAND 12/18/24 m, and reports the range.
- **Reporting:** hectares per class with the valid-pixel denominator and the sensitivity range. Show where the Sentinel-1 VV/VH water mask misses Class 2 (the L-vs-C argument).
- **Wording:** the analysis is "flooding during Jun–Nov 2026 with the Caregato breach still open." It is not presented as a mapping of the May 2024 breach. Distinguishing seasonal water from breach water is an open question, so the claim stays modest.
- **Exposure:** intersect with footprints and WorldPop for UNGRD triage counts.

### LangGraph orchestrator

- `nisar_ingestion` → parallel fan-out to `landslide_agent`, `earthquake_agent`, `flood_caregato_agent` → fan-in to `cross_hazard_synthesis_agent`.
- **Synthesis output:** a bilingual `CascadingMultiHazardBulletin` (ES/EN) plus GeoJSON for UNGRD/DAGRD, with the "SI-Assisted Analytics" badge.
- **Bulletin contract:**
  - **Placeholders, not digits.** The LLM writes ES and EN prose with placeholders such as `{eq.precision_radar}` or `{flood.class2_ha}`, referencing tool-output keys. It may not write digits.
  - **Unit and place names** (e.g. "Comuna 13") are rendered through placeholders from the ranking output; **sensor names** (Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2) are on the allow-list.
  - **Event constants** (Aug 10 2026, M7.4, depth 116 km as a USGS estimate) come from an allow-list, `validation/event_facts.json`. Each entry has its primary source: USGS event ID, SGC bulletin.
  - **Locale rendering.** A renderer fills the placeholders from the tool outputs with per-locale formatting: ES uses a decimal comma, EN a decimal point, with rounding rules per key and units appended.
  - **Check, at generation and again at serve time** (including for cached bulletins): reject the bulletin if
    - the LLM text contains any digit outside a placeholder or allow-listed constant;
    - a placeholder references a missing key;
    - the tool-output hash differs from the one the bulletin was rendered from.

### R3-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Condition 3 of the new 'beats' rule is a no-difference test ('the CI includes 0'), used as evidence of absence. The result depends on power, not on whether the control is clean. (a) Built-up and non-built pixels behave differently in coherence even without an event: urban scatterers stay coherent while open ground and pasture decorrelate. So a pre-event K pass can show a small, real, nonzero E. With thousands of pixels at 20 m and pixel-level resampling, the CI on E_K will be narrow and exclude 0. Condition 3 then fails by construction, and 'radar beats baseline' becomes unreachable regardless of the quake signal. (b) Conversely, a noisy or small K pass gives a wide CI that includes 0 and passes trivially. The headline claim depends on this rule.

**Remedy**

> Replace condition 3 with a pre-registered test that does not reward low power. Either use an equivalence margin (for example, the 95% CI of E_K lies within ±δ, with δ stated), or use a difference test (the bootstrap CI of E_C − E_K is above 0). Report E_K and its CI in every branch, and freeze the choice in validation/README.md.

### R3-2 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The CI on the built-up excess E resamples individual pixels (stratified within barrios).
